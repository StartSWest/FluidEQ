protecting, and GPL-3.0 provides for it explicitly. Section 7(e) permits a
licensor to add a term "declining to grant rights under trademark law for use
of some trade names, trademarks, or service marks". That is what this document
does, and it does nothing else. It takes away no freedom the GPL gives you.

None of this needs permission, and none of it is affected by the term below.

- Redistribute FluidEQ unchanged, under its own name, with its own logo.
- Say truthfully what your software is: "based on FluidEQ", "a fork of

- Exercise every right the GPL grants: run, study, modify, and redistribute the

## What the term declines to license

Rights under **trademark law** to use the FluidEQ name or logo:

- as the name, icon, or branding of a **modified** version you distribute to
  others;
- for a product, service, domain, store listing, or organisation of your own;
- in any way that states or suggests that the author endorses, sponsors,
  supplies, or is responsible for what you are distributing.

If you distribute a modified version, change the name and the icon. The
branding lives in one module for exactly this reason — see
`src/common/branding.ts` — and replacing it is a small, deliberate edit rather
than a hunt through the tree. That is the whole of the obligation: it costs you
nothing in source code, and it means a user who reports a bug against
"FluidEQ" is reporting it against the thing this project actually shipped.

## How this interacts with the GPL

The GPL's own words are the point here, so they are worth stating exactly.

- This is an **additional term under GPL-3.0 section 7(e)**, not a condition on
  the copyright license and not a further restriction. The code remains
  licensed under GPL-3.0-or-later with no strings attached.
- Section 7 lets you **remove this term from any material you convey**. You may
  strip the marks and this notice from your copy, and the GPL grant is
  unchanged either way.
- Where a court finds any part of this document unenforceable, it falls away
  and the GPL grant stands undisturbed.
- If this document and the license text ever appear to conflict, **the license
  text controls**.

Trademark law protects users from being misled about who made something. It
does not, and here explicitly does not, restrict the freedom to use, study,
modify or share the software.
